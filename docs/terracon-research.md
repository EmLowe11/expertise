## 2. What Terracon Sells

Terracon presents its offering three ways: **service lines**, **project lifecycle phases**, and **sectors**.

### 2.1 Service lines (four core)

1. **Geotechnical:** subsurface exploration, drilling, foundation design recommendations, geophysics, and geotechnical instrumentation. This is the historical core, backed by the 200+ rig fleet.
2. **Materials:** construction materials testing (CMT), special inspections, structural steel NDT, construction QA/QC programs, lab testing, and pavement engineering (expanded through the Transtec acquisition).
3. **Environmental:** Phase I/II environmental site assessments (ESAs), due diligence, remediation, industrial hygiene (asbestos, lead, mold, indoor air quality), air permitting, NEPA, natural and cultural resources, archaeology, and sustainability/resiliency. ENR has ranked Terracon #1 in Asbestos & Lead Abatement Design (2016).
4. **Facilities:** building enclosure and roof consulting, property condition assessments (PCAs), facility condition assessments, forensic engineering, and owner's representation. This line was strengthened heavily by the **Marx Okubo** deal (Nov 2026).

### 2.2 Project lifecycle

| Phase | What Terracon does |
|---|---|
| **Select Site** | Desktop studies (Stage1), location intelligence (Pivvot), geotechnical and environmental site screening |
| **Design & Mitigate** | Geotechnical design, environmental permitting and remediation design, materials solutions |
| **Construct** | Materials testing, inspection, QA/QC, geotechnical construction support |
| **Manage Assets** | Condition assessments, repair design, roof/enclosure maintenance, pavement management |

### 2.3 Sectors served

- **Transportation & Infrastructure:** highways, bridges, airports (e.g., Salt Lake City International redevelopment), rail, water/wastewater. A Director of Federal Infrastructure was hired in Aug 2026.
- **Power Generation & Transmission:** solar, renewables, traditional generation, transmission routing (Pivvot).
- **Industrial & Logistics:** fast-mobilizing site selection, due diligence, construction support.
- **Digital Infrastructure:** **data centers**, telecom, fiber, 5G. Terracon is expanding in Silicon Valley (San Jose office, 2026).
- **Healthcare:** hospitals, medical research facilities, senior living.
- **Federal:** brownfields, building enclosure, facility condition assessments, disaster response.
- **Commercial:** commercial real estate, multifamily, real estate transaction due diligence.

### 2.4 Proprietary products and technology

- **Compass:** client portal.
- **Stage1:** desktop site-selection study tool.
- **Pivvot:** geospatial location-intelligence platform for energy siting and routing (acquired in 2021).
- **TARGETID:** proprietary solution listed under innovation.
- **COMMAND Center™:** concrete temperature monitoring (from Transtec).
- **FactorEarth:** field data collection platform for cultural resources (from Metcalf).
- **CMELMS:** in-house laboratory management system (launched 2002).
- **Brickeye partnership (Nov 2026):** a strategic partnership with Brickeye (maker of LumiCon®) to reduce risk on mass-concrete pours nationwide.

### 2.5 Client programs

- **National Accounts Program** (since 1997): one point of contact and a dedicated National Account Manager for multi-site clients. It offers consistent delivery across regions and counts Fortune 500 companies as clients.
- **Flexible payment options** for keeping projects moving.

### 4.2 Pattern in the full history (1986 to 2026)

Terracon's acquisition history runs back to Barnett, Stuart & Associates (KS) in 1986. Its targets fall into a few repeat types:

1. **Regional geotechnical and materials testing firms** that fill in the national map. Examples: Dente (NY), ROC (NY), Earth Exploration (IN), GC&T (VA), GeoConcepts (VA), CHJ (CA), Mayes (WA/OR), Dunkelberger (FL).
2. **Drilling companies** that add rig capacity. Examples: High Plains Drilling, AquaDrill, Grant Drilling, DEI, SAGE Drilling.
3. **Environmental, industrial hygiene, and cultural resources specialists.** Examples: IHI, RGA, Argus Pacific, Liesch, ESI, Metcalf, Flat Earth.
4. **Building enclosure and facilities consultancies.** Examples: Stafford, BES, TAM, Engineered Concepts, Marx Okubo.
5. **Technology and data platforms.** Examples: Pivvot, Transtec (COMMAND Center), Metcalf (FactorEarth).
6. **Certification and licensure plays.** Examples: GeoCapitol (a D.C. Certified Business Enterprise / Local Business Enterprise) and an NY engineering license from Booz Allen.

### 4.3 How Terracon runs deals

- **Branding:** acquired firms usually keep their name with the suffix **"A Terracon Company"** for a transition period, then often fold into the Terracon brand (e.g., DEI is now "known as Terracon").
- **People first:** every employee of the acquired firm joins Terracon immediately, and press releases always promise a "seamless transition."
- **Local + national pitch:** a local brand and relationships, backed by nearby Terracon offices and national resources.
- **Culture fit:** an employee-owned acquirer appeals to founder-owned and employee-owned sellers. Marx Okubo, for example, was itself employee-owned.
- **Process:** a dedicated in-house **M&A team** handles the process "from lead identification through closure" and integration into "culture, processes, and systems."
- **Deal size:** most targets have **30 to 200 employees**. Most deals are small tuck-ins, with an occasional larger platform deal.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

Terracon sits between the mega engineering firms and the regional specialists.

**Direct competitors in geotechnical, materials testing, and environmental work:**
- ECS Group of Companies (Chantilly, VA)
- Intertek-PSI (Professional Service Industries)
- UES (Universal Engineering Sciences), backed by private equity and an active acquirer
- Braun Intertec (Minneapolis)
- Kleinfelder
- Langan
- GEI Consultants
- S&ME
- Atlas Technical Consultants (private-equity owned roll-up)
- NV5 Global
- Partner Engineering and Science (overlaps with Marx Okubo-style real estate due diligence)

**Large multidisciplinary firms that compete on bigger programs:** Jacobs, AECOM, Tetra Tech, WSP, Stantec, Kimley-Horn.

**Competitive edges Terracon claims:**
- National scale (200+ offices, 140+ labs, 200+ rigs) combined with local delivery
- A proprietary data archive built over decades of site work, plus site-selection tools
- 100% employee ownership, which helps with recruiting, retention, and seller appeal in M&A
- An in-house tech stack (Compass, Stage1, Pivvot, CMELMS)

**Industry dynamic:** the A/E/environmental testing market is consolidating quickly, and private-equity-backed buyers such as UES and Atlas compete for the same targets. Terracon's pitch to sellers is that an employee-owned buyer offers more cultural continuity than a private-equity firm.

## 6. Growth and Client-Acquisition Opportunities

Terracon wants more clients. This section lists where the research points.

### 6.1 High-momentum sectors
1. **Data centers and digital infrastructure:** large site-selection, geotechnical, and mass-concrete demand. The Brickeye mass-concrete partnership and the San Jose expansion point straight at this.
2. **Power and grid:** renewables siting, transmission routing, and energy development constraint mapping (a recent case study covered 140,000 acres). Pivvot differentiates Terracon here.
3. **Federal and transportation infrastructure:** continuing federal infrastructure spending. Terracon has a new Director of Federal Infrastructure, plus DOT relationships via Wang Engineering.
4. **Real estate capital markets:** Marx Okubo brings lender, investor, and owner relationships for PCAs and due diligence. Terracon can cross-sell Phase I ESAs, geotechnical, and roof/enclosure work to them.
5. **Site investigation and remediation:** a new national director role was created in Nov 2026 to grow this specialty.
6. **Education and healthcare:** steady recurring work for facilities, IH, and CMT.

### 6.2 Cross-sell plays
- Use the National Accounts Program for Fortune 500 multi-site owners in retail, logistics, QSR, telecom, and data centers.
- Bundle the full lifecycle (site selection, design, construction testing, asset management) to deepen wallet share with each client.
- Push acquired-firm clients into the wider Terracon catalog.

### 6.3 Ideal client profiles (for prospecting)
- Developers and REITs (industrial, multifamily, data center)
- Hyperscalers and colocation data center operators
- Utilities, IPPs, and renewable developers
- State DOTs, airports, and transit authorities
- Federal agencies (USACE, GSA, DoD installations)
- Healthcare systems and universities
- Lenders, CMBS servicers, and real estate investors (via Marx Okubo)
- General contractors and design-build teams that need CMT and special inspections

## 7. Implications for the Planned M&A Application

The research points to features the app should support, matched to how Terracon already does deals.

### 7.1 Target sourcing and scoring
- **Target database:** firm name, HQ, office locations, headcount, service lines (geo, materials, environmental, facilities, drilling, cultural, tech), sectors, ownership type (founder, ESOP, private equity), licenses and certifications (DBE/CBE, state PE licenses), lab accreditations (AASHTO, ISO 17025, etc.), and rig count.
- **Fit scoring** based on Terracon's past patterns:
  - *Geographic gap fill:* overlap or adjacency with Terracon's 200+ offices.
  - *Capability gap fill:* adds a service line or deepens a thin one (e.g., cultural resources, pavement, facilities).
  - *Size band:* about 25 to 250 employees, with a flag for larger platform deals.
  - *Culture fit:* founder-led or employee-owned, signs of succession need, owner age.
  - *Strategic sector exposure:* data centers, power, federal, transportation.
  - *Tech or IP value:* proprietary software or data.
- **Competitive threat flag:** watch for private-equity-backed rivals (UES, Atlas, etc.) circling the same targets.

### 7.2 Pipeline and deal management
- Stages: Lead → Outreach → NDA → Diligence → LOI → Close → Integration.
- Owner and relationship tracking (many deals start from teaming relationships; DEI, for example, had a "long history of collaboration").
- Document checklist: financials, backlog, client concentration, licenses, insurance and claims history, and equipment (rigs, lab gear).

### 7.3 Integration tracking
- "A Terracon Company" branding timeline and the eventual rebrand
- Systems migration (CMELMS, Compass, HR/ESOP enrollment)
- Retention of key staff and clients, plus cross-sell revenue after close
- Integration health metrics for the M&A team (Terracon markets its integration quality, so measure it)

### 7.4 Map and portfolio views
- National map overlaying Terracon offices, labs, and acquired firms against prospective targets
- A timeline of 80+ acquisitions (the seed data is in Section 4 and in Terracon's companies page)

### 7.5 Seed data available now
- The full list of acquired companies with join years (Terracon Companies page)
- Terracon's sector and service-line taxonomy, which can drive the target-tagging scheme
